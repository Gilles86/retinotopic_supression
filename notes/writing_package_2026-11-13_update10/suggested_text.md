## Results, section 5: the gain terms along the visual hierarchy

The target gain increased along the visual hierarchy. Per participant, we regressed each area's gain on the log of the
area's pRF size. The target gain rose by 27 percentage points per doubling of pRF size ([15, 68], positive in 22 of 28
participants, p = .002). This held in the dorsal (V1–V3, V3AB, IPS; p = .011) and the occipitotemporal stream (V1–V3,
hV4, VO, LO, TO; p = .003), with a rank ordering of the areas instead of pRF size (p = .008), and with Gaussian and DN
pRFs (Table S). The sustained gain shared by the four locations also became more negative in higher areas (27 of 28
participants). The HP-specific sustained reduction did not: dS did not change with pRF size (−0.7 percentage points
per doubling [−3.7, 3.3], p = .61), and neither did its transient counterparts (dD p = .63, dT p = .68). The target
gain grew more than dS (paired p = .018). A slope of dS larger than 4.6 percentage points per doubling would have been
detected with 80% power, so dS does not accumulate along the hierarchy as the target gain does. dS is small, though
(about 1%), and relative to its own size the data cannot tell whether it grows (p = .49 for the comparison with the
target gain in these units).

dS was closer to a constant amount than to a fixed fraction of the shared sustained gain. The ratio of the two was
close to 1 in V1, V3 and hV4 and about 0.2 in V3AB, VO, LO and TO, and across areas within participants dS did not
follow the shared sustained gain (slope 0.22 [−0.28, 0.56], p = .63). Within areas, the target gain was larger in
voxels with larger pRFs (7 percentage points per doubling, p = .007), but within an area larger pRFs also lie
further from the fovea, so this does not separate pRF size from eccentricity; dS showed no such trend (p = .18).
Finally, the width of the sustained field did not scale with pRF size (−0.04° per doubling [−0.32, 0.10] among fits
with a clearly reliable sustained gain; Fig. S, d).

## Discussion paragraph

Each area was fitted with the stimulus as its input, so an area's gain is the gain accumulated up to that area, not
the gain that area adds. Read this way, the target gain is built up along both streams: it is three to four times
larger in the areas with the largest pRFs than in V1. The sustained reduction at the high-probability location
behaves differently. It is already present in V3 and hV4, it does not grow in higher areas, it is closer to a fixed
amount than to a fixed fraction of the sustained gain that is shared by all four locations, and its spatial extent
does not follow the pRF size of the area. This pattern fits a reduction that is set up early in the visual hierarchy
and passed on, or one applied with the same spatial profile at several stages, better than one that accumulates from
stage to stage like the target gain. The estimate is small, however, and the data cannot exclude that it grows in
proportion to its own size; with DN pRFs it was somewhat larger in higher areas (not significant after correction).
