**Figure 5. The gain fields: gain at the target and the distractor, and less sustained gain at the high-probability (HP) location.** Gain-field model fits with DoG pRFs, the pRFs refitted together with the gain fields (n = 28); gains are in % (the change in drive at the centre of a field: +30%, 30% more); differences between gains (HP − LP) are in percentage points of gain, written as %. a, The fitted parametric gain field over the search display, not data: for each area, the median over participants (n = 28) of each participant's fitted parameters, put through the model's field equations. The display is rotated so that the HP location is at the top (purple dashed ring); small circles, the eight item positions on the 4° ring (dotted; stronger: the four possible target and distractor locations); grey circle, the 3.17° aperture of the mapping bar; +, fixation. Left three maps, the sustained field at HP minus at the low-probability (LP) locations: only the HP-specific part of the sustained field, the area's median HP − LP difference estimated alone (dS, the value panel c plots, printed in bold next to HP) times the field's Gaussian at HP (width: the area's median full width at half maximum among fits whose sustained gain is reliably different from zero, |gain| > 2 SE; Supplementary Fig., field widths); thin purple line, the half−maximum (FWHM) contour of that field. One scale for the three areas, white (no difference) to deep purple at the deepest area's HP − LP (−2.7%; no headroom, so that area saturates). The sustained field itself is a suppression at all four possible locations in most areas (7 of 9: V3, V3AB, IPS, hV4, VO, LO, TO); the grey lines under each map give the sustained gain at LP and at HP (median joint-fit LP gain; at HP, that plus the HP − LP difference drawn; with the pRFs refitted, the level shared by all four locations trades off against the item weight and the pRF amplitude, so read its sign, not its size). Right three maps, the transient fields of the target (orange, around the orange diamond, at an LP location, here opposite HP) and of the distractor (green, around the green dot, drawn at an LP location next to HP) in one map; values, the target gain of panel b and the distractor gain averaged over its locations, as in panel b (the transient HP − LP difference is in Supplementary Fig., transient HP − LP); both on one scale, 0 to the largest value, so their colours compare directly. The transient fields exist only while a search display is on; their fitted width is often at a bound of the fit (0.5° or 8°), so it is set for display to the same FWHM of 3.2° in every area and is not interpreted (field widths per participant: Supplementary Fig., field widths). Values used: V3: sustained gain at LP −0.4%, at HP −3.1% (HP − LP = dS estimated alone −2.7%, the median of panel c; joint-fit dS −9.5%), width σ 1.14° (FWHM 2.7°, median of the 17 fits with a reliable sustained gain; all fits 4.1°); target gain +33%; distractor gain +6%; hV4: sustained gain at LP −11%, at HP −13% (HP − LP = dS estimated alone −2.4%, the median of panel c; joint-fit dS −5.6%), width σ 1.21° (FWHM 2.9°, median of the 19 fits with a reliable sustained gain; all fits 3.0°); target gain +59%; distractor gain +21%; TO: sustained gain at LP −67%, at HP −68% (HP − LP = dS estimated alone −1.9%, the median of panel c; joint-fit dS −3.1%), width σ 1.12° (FWHM 2.6°, median of the 26 fits with a reliable sustained gain; all fits 2.6°); target gain +18%; distractor gain +8%. The sustained gain is reliably lower at HP than at LP (q < .05) in V3 and hV4 in the joint fit and, estimated alone as drawn, in V3, hV4 and TO (panel c). The sum of the three fields during a search display is in Supplementary Fig. (gain field during search). b, c: dots are subjects (triangles, beyond the axis), points and bars the median and bootstrapped 95% CI; stars, Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 0, Benjamini-Hochberg across the nine ROIs, * q < .05, ** q < .01, *** q < .001 (exact p and q per ROI in the parameter table). b, Gain at the target (orange) and at the distractor (green, averaged over its locations) while the search display is on, all terms fitted jointly. The gain against pRF size is in a supplementary figure. c, leftmost and larger: all areas pooled (per participant the median dS over the nine areas; median over participants −1.0% [−1.9, −0.4], negative in 22 of 28 participants, Wilcoxon p = 2.7 × 10⁻⁴); the claim rests on this pooled effect, consistent across participants, rather than on any single area. Not reliable in any single pathway (the same pooling per pathway, BH across the three: early −0.5% (q = .057), dorsal −0.5% (q = .578), occipitotemporal −1.3% (q = .089)). Right of the divider, the nine areas as before: dS = g_HP − g_LP (in percentage points), the sustained gain at the HP location minus that at the low-probability (LP) locations, each term estimated alone on the item model with sustained gain (conditional estimate). Estimated alone (c): dS: reliable in V3 −2.7% (q = 0.036), hV4 −2.4% (q = 0.036), TO −1.9% (q = 0.036); per-subject median over ROIs −1.0% [−1.9%, −0.4%], 79% of subjects < 0, p = 0.00027. Fitted jointly with all other terms: dS q < .05 in V3 −9.5% (q = 0.034), hV4 −5.6% (q = 0.024); per-subject median over ROIs −3.5% [−6.7%, −1.3%], 79% of subjects < 0, p = 0.0044. The measured responses at the item locations (Fig. 3) are lower at HP for every item type, without an item-specific difference.
