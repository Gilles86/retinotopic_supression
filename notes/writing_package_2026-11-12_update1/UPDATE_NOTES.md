## What to do in the manuscript

1. Methods, participants: add handedness (26 right-handed, 2 left-handed).
2. Model vs measured item responses: do not write that the model "explains only half" of the HP reduction. The
   predicted HP − LP (−5.4%) lies inside the measured 95% CI (−11.7% [−19.4, −3.9]; gap p = .095). Suggested
   wording: "The gain-field model accounts for the reduced response at the HP location relative to its neighbours,
   carried by the sustained HP field, but not for the lower response next to HP than opposite it, a gradient present
   in the measured responses and in behaviour but outside what a single HP-centred field predicts." (next − opposite
   gap −13.6 ± 6.2%, p = .040.)
3. Behaviour next to HP vs Richter et al.: the pattern (distractor next to HP slowest, opposite about as fast as HP,
   target cost graded HP > next > opposite) is already present in session 1 block 1, before any location had been HP
   (+34.4 ms next − opposite, p = .025), and does not change with history (interaction p ≥ .79). Suggested wording:
   "Unlike Richter et al., a distractor next to the HP location slowed responses more than one opposite it, and this
   was already the case before any location had been HP (first block)." Geometry is not the reason (same layout
   relative to eccentricity); paradigm differences are the continuous bar sweep, no placeholder cue, no omission
   trials, and HP 62.5% vs ~57% of distractor trials.
