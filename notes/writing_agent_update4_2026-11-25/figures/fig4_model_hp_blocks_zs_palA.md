**Figure 4. Gain-field model.** a, A search display on screen is reduced to the model input: the bar and the eight items as patches of the same size and orientation (their colour, and so which items are the target and the distractor, are not in the input). The weight of the items relative to the bar is fitted per area. Each voxel's pRF reads this input multiplied by a gain field: 1 plus a sustained field at the four possible target and distractor locations, plus transient fields at the target (g_T) and at the distractor (g_D), present while a search display is on. The sustained field sums to zero over the four locations (g_HP + 3 g_LP = 0): it captures only how the HP location differs from the other three, so less gain at HP (purple) comes with slightly more at each LP location (gold). The pRFs, the HRF and the gains are fitted together. HP is at the lower right (purple dashed ring). All gains are illustrative and exaggerated for clarity. b, A schematic stretch of a run for one pRF (charcoal) near a ring location, in a block with HP near it and in a block with HP at the opposite location. One bar sweep from left to right and four illustrative trials, the same in both blocks, so that only the sustained gain differs (in the experiment the distractor is also more frequent at HP). Symbols above the onsets show the screen (target: diamond; distractor: dot; HP: purple ring). Rows show the stimulus reaching the pRF (bar and items), the gain it sees (purple: the sustained field, present for the whole block; less gain with HP near, slightly more with HP at another location), and the predicted BOLD response after the HRF, with and without gain; coloured arrows mark where each gain changes the prediction. The dotted line marks the peak response in the block with HP not near, in both blocks; with HP near, the response peaks lower (purple arrow). With HP near, the item responses are lowered too: each item's own gain lifts its response (orange, green arrows from below) and the sustained loss at HP pushes it down (purple arrows from above); they meet on the predicted response. The size of the HP reduction is exaggerated for clarity. Rows share their scale across columns.
