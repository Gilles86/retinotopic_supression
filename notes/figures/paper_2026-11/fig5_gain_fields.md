**Figure 5. The gain fields: gain at the target and the distractor, and less sustained gain at the high-probability (HP) location.** Gain-field model fits with DoG pRFs (n = 30); a gain is the fractional change in drive at the centre of a field (+0.30: 30% more). a, c, d: dots are subjects (triangles, beyond the axis), points and bars the median and bootstrapped 95% CI; stars, Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 0, Benjamini-Hochberg across the nine ROIs, * q < .05, ** q < .01, *** q < .001 (exact p and q per ROI in the parameter table). a, Gain at the target (orange) and at the distractor (blue, averaged over its locations) while the search display is on, all terms fitted jointly. The gain against pRF size is in a supplementary figure. b, The sustained gain field over the search display, drawn to scale (red shading): one Gaussian per item (white discs, 4° eccentricity, neighbouring items 5.7° apart; grey circle, the 3.17° aperture of the bar stimulus), with less gain at the HP than at the LP locations (the HP − LP difference exaggerated for display). Dashed circle: the full width at half maximum (FWHM = 2.355 σ) of the fitted sustained field, the median over all subjects and ROIs (4.8°; per-ROI medians: V1 5.6°; V2 6.4°; V3 5.6°; V3AB 4.6°; IPS 6.6°; hV4 3.9°; VO 4.3°; LO 4.6°; TO 3.7°; 12% of the fits at a bound of σ, 0.5° or 8°). The field reaches beyond the item and overlaps those of its neighbours. c, dS = g_HP − g_LP, the sustained gain at the HP location minus that at the low-probability (LP) locations, each term estimated alone on the item model with sustained gain (conditional estimate). d, The gain analogue of Fig. 1c, d: dD = g_HP_dyn − g_LP_dyn, the gain the distractor draws at the HP location minus at an LP location (blue), next to dT, the target gain at the HP location minus at an LP location (orange), each estimated alone as in c; the display icons are those of Fig. 1 (HP ringed in red; the item at HP minus at an LP location). Less gain for a distractor at HP in V3AB, IPS, hV4 and VO; the target gain shows no reliable HP − LP difference after correction (smallest q = .07, V3; a positive trend in IPS, q = .10), and none when fitted jointly with all other terms either (q ≥ .36). Compared directly (per subject dD − dT, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Benjamini-Hochberg across ROIs), the distractor and target HP − LP terms do not differ reliably in any ROI (V3 +.053, p = .021, q = .094; IPS −.097, p = .012, q = .094; all others p ≥ .36, q = .855; pooled over ROIs per subject, median −.005, p = .89); the target series is shown for context. Stars per series (against 0), one row each. When dS and dD are fitted together with all other terms (supplementary figure and parameter table), no ROI survives correction: dD stays at −7 to −10% in V3AB, IPS, hV4 and VO (q ≈ .09–.15), dS is −4 to −5% in V1–V3 (q ≥ .07). The measured responses at the item locations (Fig. 3) show less response at HP for every item type, significant in the early areas, without a distractor-specific difference.
