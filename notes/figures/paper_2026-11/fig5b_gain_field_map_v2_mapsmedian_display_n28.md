**Figure 5b. The fitted gain field over the visual field.** The joint gain-field fit's parametric field, not data: for each participant (n = 28) the fitted parameters of the area were put through the model's field equations, and the maps show the median over participants at each point. The three areas are those where the sustained gain is reliably lower at HP than at the other three locations at n = 28 (joint fit, HP − LP: V3 −.090, q = .032; hV4 −.057, q = .028; TO −.024, q = .044; Wilcoxon signed-rank across participants, Benjamini-Hochberg across the nine areas; V1 −.071, q = .20, and the other areas are in the parameter table). Colour: % change in the gain on the stimulus drive (magma: dark, the strongest suppression; bright, no change or enhancement), on each area's own scale (colour bar under the column); the teal contour and the teal tick on the colour bar mark no change (0%), and thin grey contours mark fixed steps. The display is rotated so that the high-probability (HP) distractor location is at the top (red dashed ring); small circles, the eight item positions on the 4° ring (stronger: the four possible target and distractor locations; the two next to HP at the left and right, the one opposite HP at the bottom); dashed circle, the 3.17° aperture of the mapping bar; +, fixation. Top row, the sustained field between search displays: a Gaussian gain field at each of the four locations, with its own gain at HP and a common gain at the other three. Middle row, during a search display with the distractor at HP (blue dot) and the target opposite HP (orange diamond): the sustained field plus the transient fields at the distractor and at the target. The transient width is at a bound of the fit (0.5° or 8°) in about half of the participants per area, so the shape of the transient fields is approximate. Bottom: the same field around the 4° item ring, by angle from HP (0°: HP; ±90°: next to HP; ±180°: opposite HP) (solid, sustained field; dashed, during the display). The sustained field narrows up the hierarchy (median width σ: V3 1.9°, hV4 1.3°, TO 1.1°; FWHM 4.6°, 3.0° and 2.5°) while pRFs grow, so the field's effect on a voxel's response, which integrates the field over its pRF, is broader than the field drawn here in visual space. The measured item responses of Fig. 3 are not drawn on the profiles: they are % of the mean BOLD response at pRF-selected voxels, not % change in gain. Field around the ring: V3 (n = 28): sustained field at HP −10.9%, next to HP +0.3%, opposite +0.3%; during the display +1.4%, −3.2% and +23.9%; hV4 (n = 28): sustained field at HP −12.8%, next to HP −5.8%, opposite −5.8%; during the display +7.1%, −8.0% and +50.1%; TO (n = 28): sustained field at HP −47.6%, next to HP −35.4%, opposite −35.4%; during the display −13.8%, −33.1% and +13.3%.
