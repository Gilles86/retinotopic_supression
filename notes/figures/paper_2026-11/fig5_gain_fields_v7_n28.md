**Figure 5. The gain fields: gain at the target and the distractor, and less sustained gain at the high-probability (HP) location.** Gain-field model fits with DoG pRFs (n = 28); a gain is the fractional change in drive at the centre of a field (+0.30: 30% more). a, The fitted parametric gain field over the search display, not data: for each area, the median over participants (n = 28) of each participant's joint-fit parameter (all terms fitted together), put through the model's field equations; value, the fractional change in gain on the stimulus drive (−0.10: 10% less). The display is rotated so that the HP location is at the top (red dashed ring); small circles, the eight item positions on the 4° ring (dotted; stronger: the four possible target and distractor locations); grey circle, the 3.17° aperture of the mapping bar; +, fixation. Top row, the sustained field, present throughout the block: a Gaussian field at each of the four possible locations, with the median gain at the low-probability (LP) locations and, at HP, that gain plus the median HP − LP difference of the joint fit, so that the HP − LP difference printed on each map is the joint dS of panel c's legend and the text (panel c shows dS estimated alone, which is smaller); width, the area's median fitted width. The sustained gain is negative at all four locations in most areas (7 of 9: V3, V3AB, IPS, hV4, VO, LO, TO). Bottom row, the transient fields of the target (orange, around the orange diamond, at an LP location, here opposite HP) and of the distractor (blue, around the blue dot, drawn at an LP location next to HP) in one map; values, the target gain of panel b and the distractor gain averaged over its locations, as in panel b (the transient HP − LP difference is in panel d); both on one scale, so their colours compare directly. The transient fields exist only while a search display is on; their fitted width is often at a bound of the fit (0.5° or 8°), so it is set for display to the same FWHM of 3.2° in every area and is not interpreted (field widths per subject: Supplementary Fig. S, field widths). Colour scales: sustained row: each area on its own scale (V3 −0.10 to 0.00; hV4 −0.12 to 0.00; TO −0.38 to 0.00); transient row: target (orange) and distractor (blue) on one scale, 0 to +0.60, for both and all three areas (magma for the sustained field, teal line at no change; the transient fields from white, no change, to orange, target, and blue, distractor). Values used: V3: sustained gain at LP 0.00, at HP −0.09 (HP − LP = joint dS −0.09; median g_HP −0.14), width σ 1.94° (FWHM 4.6°); target gain +0.30; distractor gain +0.03; hV4: sustained gain at LP −0.06, at HP −0.12 (HP − LP = joint dS −0.06; median g_HP −0.16), width σ 1.29° (FWHM 3.0°); target gain +0.56; distractor gain +0.22; TO: sustained gain at LP −0.35, at HP −0.38 (HP − LP = joint dS −0.02; median g_HP −0.48), width σ 1.07° (FWHM 2.5°); target gain +0.22; distractor gain +0.08. V3, hV4 and TO are the areas where the sustained gain is reliably lower at HP than at LP in the joint fit (q < .05; V1, V2 and the other areas are in the parameter table). The sum of the three fields during a search display is in Supplementary Fig. S (gain field during search). b–d: dots are subjects (triangles, beyond the axis), points and bars the median and bootstrapped 95% CI; stars, Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 0, Benjamini-Hochberg across the nine ROIs, * q < .05, ** q < .01, *** q < .001 (exact p and q per ROI in the parameter table). b, Gain at the target (orange) and at the distractor (blue, averaged over its locations) while the search display is on, all terms fitted jointly. The gain against pRF size is in a supplementary figure. c, dS = g_HP − g_LP, the sustained gain at the HP location minus that at the low-probability (LP) locations, each term estimated alone on the item model with sustained gain (conditional estimate). d, The gain analogue of Fig. 1c, d: dD = g_HP_dyn − g_LP_dyn, the gain the distractor draws at the HP location minus at an LP location (blue), next to dT, the target gain at the HP location minus at an LP location (orange), each estimated alone as in c; the display icons are those of Fig. 1 (HP ringed in red; the item at HP minus at an LP location). Estimated alone (c, d): dS: reliable in V3 −0.030 (q = 0.046), TO −0.019 (q = 0.035); per-subject median over ROIs −0.014 [−0.018, −0.006], 79% of subjects < 0, p = 0.00011. The distractor term (dD): reliable in LO −0.033 (q = 0.022); per-subject median over ROIs −0.010 [−0.029, +0.005], 61% of subjects < 0, p = 0.099. The target term (dT): no ROI q < .05 (smallest q = 0.092, V3); per-subject median over ROIs −0.034 [−0.068, +0.005], 68% of subjects < 0, p = 0.018. Their difference (dD − dT): no ROI q < .05 (smallest q = 0.47, TO); per-subject median over ROIs +0.009 [−0.004, +0.052], 36% of subjects < 0, p = 0.24. Fitted jointly with all other terms: dS q < .05 in V3 −0.090 (q = 0.032), hV4 −0.057 (q = 0.028), TO −0.024 (q = 0.044); per-subject median over ROIs −0.034 [−0.066, −0.015], 79% of subjects < 0, p = 0.0027; dD no ROI q < .05 (smallest 0.29, V3); per-subject median over ROIs +0.004 [−0.041, +0.120], 46% of subjects < 0, p = 0.29. The measured responses at the item locations (Fig. 3) are lower at HP for every item type, without an item-specific difference.
