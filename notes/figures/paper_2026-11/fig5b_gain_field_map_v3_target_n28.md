**Figure 5b. The fitted gain field over the visual field.** The joint gain-field fit's parametric field, not data: the median over participants (n = 28) of each fitted parameter of the area (gains and widths), put through the model's field equations. V3, hV4 and TO are the areas where the sustained gain is reliably lower at HP than at the other three locations at n = 28 (joint fit, HP − LP: V3 −.090, q = .032; hV4 −.057, q = .028; TO −.024, q = .044; Wilcoxon signed-rank across participants, Benjamini-Hochberg across the nine areas); V1 is shown for comparison: its estimate is of similar size but not reliable (−.071, q = .20); the other areas are in the parameter table. Colour: % change in the gain on the stimulus drive (magma: dark, the strongest suppression; bright, no change or enhancement), on each area's own scale (colour bar under the column); the teal contour and the teal tick on the colour bar mark no change (0%), and thin grey contours mark fixed steps. The display is rotated so that the high-probability (HP) distractor location is at the top (red dashed ring); small circles, the eight item positions on the 4° ring (stronger: the four possible target and distractor locations; the two next to HP at the left and right, the one opposite HP at the bottom); dashed circle, the 3.17° aperture of the mapping bar; +, fixation. Top row, the sustained field between search displays: a Gaussian gain field at each of the four locations, with its own gain at HP and a common gain at the other three. Middle row, during a search display with the target opposite HP (orange diamond): the sustained field plus the transient target field (median target gain). The transient width is at a bound of the fit (0.5° or 8°) in about half of the participants per area, so the target field is drawn with the median width of the participants whose width is not at a bound (V1 2.8° (from 16 of 28); V3 2.6° (from 13 of 28); hV4 2.0° (from 9 of 28); TO 1.4° (from 13 of 28)). The transient distractor field is not drawn: in the joint fit its median gain is positive (higher at HP than at LP in V3 and hV4), whereas the HP − LP distractor gain estimated alone is slightly negative and not reliable (see Fig. 5d), so its drawn shape would reflect the joint fit's trade-offs rather than a finding. Bottom: the same field around the 4° item ring, by angle from HP (0°: HP; ±90°: next to HP; ±180°: opposite HP) (solid, sustained field; dashed, during the display). The sustained field narrows up the hierarchy (median width σ: V1 2.2°, V3 1.9°, hV4 1.3°, TO 1.1°; FWHM 5.2°, 4.6°, 3.0° and 2.5°) while pRFs grow, so the field's effect on a voxel's response, which integrates the field over its pRF, is broader than the field drawn here in visual space. The measured item responses of Fig. 3 are not drawn on the profiles: they are % of the mean BOLD response at pRF-selected voxels, not % change in gain. Field around the ring: V1 (n = 28): sustained field at HP −4.1%, next to HP +2.7%, opposite +3.0%; during the display −4.1%, +2.5% and +1.1%; V3 (n = 28): sustained field at HP −13.8%, next to HP −0.6%, opposite −0.5%; during the display −13.6%, +2.1% and +29.5%; hV4 (n = 28): sustained field at HP −16.0%, next to HP −5.8%, opposite −5.8%; during the display −16.0%, −4.9% and +50.0%; TO (n = 28): sustained field at HP −47.6%, next to HP −35.4%, opposite −35.4%; during the display −47.6%, −35.4% and −13.7%.
