**Figure 5b. The fitted gain field, taken apart.** The joint gain-field fit's parametric field over the visual field, not data: for each area the median over participants (n = 28) of each fitted parameter, put through the model's field equations; colour, % change in the gain on the stimulus drive, each map on its own scale (colour bar under it): rows 1 and 4 in magma (dark, lower gain; bright, higher; teal line and tick, no change), rows 2 and 3, one component alone, from white (no change) to the component's colour (higher gain; grey, lower); values printed at the item locations. The display is rotated so that the high-probability (HP) distractor location is at the top (red dashed ring); circles, the eight item positions on the 4° ring (stronger: the four possible target and distractor locations); dashed circle, the 3.17° aperture of the mapping bar; +, fixation. Row 1, learned, between search displays: the sustained field, a Gaussian gain field at each of the four possible locations with its own gain at HP and a common gain at the other three (median width σ: V1 2.2°; V3 1.9°; hV4 1.3°; TO 1.1°). Row 2, the target's transient field alone, with the target (orange diamond) at the location opposite HP; it is present only while a search display is on. Row 3, the distractor's transient field alone, with the distractor (blue dot) at HP; drawn pale because it is not a reliable HP effect: in the joint fit the median distractor gain is small and similar at HP and at the other locations (V1 +0.04 at HP, −0.04 at LP; V3 +0.09 at HP, −0.00 at LP; hV4 +0.30 at HP, +0.21 at LP; TO +0.09 at HP, +0.09 at LP), and the HP − LP distractor gain estimated alone is slightly negative and not reliable (Fig. 5d). Row 4, during a search display with the distractor at HP and the target opposite: the sum of rows 1–3, as in the model's forward pass, where the transient fields are added to the sustained gain. The transient width is at a bound of the fit (0.5° or 8°) in about half of the participants, so the target and distractor fields are drawn with the median width of the participants whose width is not at a bound (V1 2.8° (from 16 of 28); V3 2.6° (from 13 of 28); hV4 2.0° (from 9 of 28); TO 1.4° (from 13 of 28); target and distractor share one width in the fit). V1 is shown for comparison: its sustained HP − LP gain is of similar size to V3's but not reliable (q = .20; V3 q = .032, hV4 .028, TO .044; Wilcoxon signed-rank across participants, Benjamini-Hochberg across nine areas). Values: V1 (n = 28): sustained field −4% at HP, +3% at the other locations; target field −2% at the target; distractor field +4% at HP (gain +0.04; at LP -0.04); during the display 0% at HP and +1% at the target; V3 (n = 28): sustained field −14% at HP, 0% at the other locations; target field +30% at the target; distractor field +9% at HP (gain +0.09; at LP -0.00); during the display −4% at HP and +30% at the target; hV4 (n = 28): sustained field −16% at HP, −6% at the other locations; target field +56% at the target; distractor field +30% at HP (gain +0.30; at LP +0.21); during the display +14% at HP and +50% at the target; TO (n = 28): sustained field −48% at HP, −35% at the other locations; target field +22% at the target; distractor field +9% at HP (gain +0.09; at LP +0.09); during the display −39% at HP and −14% at the target.
