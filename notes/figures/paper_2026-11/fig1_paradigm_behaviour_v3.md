**Figure 1. Paradigm and behaviour.** a, Search display with the bar in its 3.17° aperture: 8 items on a ring at 4°, with target and distractor at the four diagonal positions; percentages are over all trials. The target has the odd orientation (participants report the dot on it) and never appears at the distractor's location; there is no distractor on 20% of trials. b, Two trials to scale, with the bar position every 2 s below; the bar sweeps the aperture in four directions (rightward, leftward, downward, upward; 42 s each with 4.8 s rest, the sequence twice per run), and the HP location across blocks of runs (example order). c, Response time with the distractor at the HP location, at an LP location, or absent. d, Response time with the target at the HP or an LP location, on trials with a distractor. e, f, Response time when the target (e) or the distractor (f) appears at the same location as on the previous trial, or at another location; distractor repeats are compared within HP and within LP trials and averaged. Icons below each condition: the four possible locations, with HP ringed in red where the condition is defined by it (c, d); filled, the item on this trial, hollow, on the previous trial (e, f). c-f, n = 30, correct trials. Upper rows: dots are subjects, triangles are subjects beyond the axis limits; large points and bars are the mean ± within-subject SEM (Cousineau-Morey); the brackets give the median within-subject difference and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (** p < .01, *** p < .001). Lower rows: the within-subject differences of the brackets above (one dot per subject; diamond and bar, the median and its bootstrapped 95% CI, 5000 resamples), with the number of subjects on the side of the median; every strip has the same scale.
