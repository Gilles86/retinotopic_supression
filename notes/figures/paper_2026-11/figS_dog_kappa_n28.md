A centre/surround split of the sustained field is consistent in direction but too small to help prediction.** a, Schematic of the variant (model m8_full_dogk): a one-dimensional cut through a DoG pRF at the HP location. The sustained gain field F (at the four item locations; drawn here at HP, with its FWHM) multiplies the pRF's centre (driving) pooling by 1 + (1 + κ)F and its surround (suppressive) pooling by 1 + (1 − κ)F, one κ per subject × area shared by all voxels. κ = 0 is the joint fit (m7) exactly; κ < 0 puts more of the field on the surround. Dashed: centre and surround without the field; solid: with a suppressive field at HP (illustrative values; κ = −0.6 exaggerated for display). b, κ per area from the full-data fits (n = 28 participants; per area the subject × area pairs with a fit). c, Held-out ΔR² of the model with κ minus the joint fit without it, with the pRFs frozen and run-3 cross-validation (mean over the three folds), × 10⁻⁴ (27 participants; no run-3 fits for sub-08). b, c: dots are participants (triangles: beyond the axis), diamonds and bars the median and its bootstrapped 95% CI (10 000 resamples); Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 0, Benjamini-Hochberg across the nine areas, * q < .05, ** q < .01, *** q < .001.

Leave-one-subject-out (each participant scored at the median κ of the other participants, all voxels of the area, ΔR² on signal voxels): ΔR² higher in 68% of 28 participants (Wilcoxon p = 0.043; median ΔR² +3.4e−07, median δ −0.009), and the participants' data move the way the others' κ predicts in 93% (sign-flip contrast, p = 2.5e−07). The tilt is consistent in direction (more of the HP effect on the suppressive surround) but too small to help prediction: on held-out runs it lowers R² in every area, and across participants its gain is of the order of 10⁻⁷.
