**Figure 5. Less sustained gain at the location where the distractor is likely.** a, Sketch of the result, drawn to scale: the sustained gain field over the search display (red shading), one Gaussian per item (white discs, 4° eccentricity, neighbouring items 5.7° apart; grey circle, the 3.17° aperture of the bar stimulus), with less gain at the high-probability (HP) than at the low-probability (LP) locations; the HP − LP difference is exaggerated for display. The dashed circle is the full width at half maximum (FWHM = 2.355 σ) of the fitted sustained field, the median over all subjects and ROIs (4.8°; joint m7 fit, DoG pRFs); per-ROI medians: V1 5.6°; V2 6.4°; V3 5.6°; V3AB 4.6°; IPS 6.6°; hV4 3.9°; VO 4.3°; LO 4.6°; TO 3.7°. 12% of the fits sit at a bound of σ (0.5° or 8°) and constrain the width poorly. The field reaches well beyond the item and overlaps those of its neighbours, so the lower gain at HP is not confined to the item location. b, dS = g_HP − g_LP, the sustained gain at the HP location minus that at the LP locations, per ROI, each term estimated alone on m4 (conditional estimate, DoG pRFs): dots are subjects (n = 30; triangles, beyond the axis), points and bars the median and bootstrapped 95% CI. Stars: Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 0, Benjamini-Hochberg across ROIs; * q < .05, ** q < .01, *** q < .001. Squares under the data (New subjects): leave-one-subject-out test per ROI, the term added with the size estimated from the other 29 subjects (+δ) vs the opposite size (−δ); filled, a majority of subjects predicted better with +δ (one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Benjamini-Hochberg q < .05 across ROIs); open, not significant. c, The same in the measured responses, without a model, per pathway (early, V1–V3; dorsal, V3AB and IPS; occipitotemporal, hV4, VO, LO and TO): deconvolved response (nideconv) of voxels whose pRF centre lies within 2° of an item location, when that location is HP (red) vs LP (grey), all items (target, distractor, neutral) pooled and ROIs pooled within a pathway (weighted by voxel count); mean ± SEM over subjects. The HP − LP difference averaged over the shaded window (3.2–6.4 s), Wilcoxon signed-rank test against 0, the percentage beside the curves giving it relative to the LP response: early, −.046% signal (−10% of the LP response), p = .015, n = 30; dorsal, −.068% signal (−9% of the LP response), p = .115, n = 28; occipitotemporal, −.017% signal (−2% of the LP response), p = .382, n = 30. Only the early areas show a significantly lower response at HP (*); in the dorsal and occipitotemporal areas the difference has the same sign but is not significant. d, The transient distractor gain, dD = g_HP_dyn − g_LP_dyn: the gain the distractor draws when it appears at the HP location minus when it appears at an LP location, estimated alone as in b. It is negative in V3AB, IPS, hV4 and VO. When dD is fitted together with dS and the other terms (joint m7 fit, supplementary figure), no ROI survives correction: dD stays at −7 to −10% in V3AB, IPS, hV4 and VO (Benjamini-Hochberg q ≈ .09–.15) and is positive in V1–V3, while dS is −4 to −5% in V1–V3 (q ≥ .07) and close to 0 elsewhere; over all nine ROIs (median per subject) dD is not distinguishable from 0 (57% of subjects below 0, p = .44).
