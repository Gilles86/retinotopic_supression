**Figure 2. Gain-field model.** a, A search display on screen is reduced to the model input: the bar and the eight items as patches of the same size and orientation (their colour, and so which item is the singleton, is not in the input). The weight of the items relative to the bar is fitted per ROI. Each voxel's pRF, fixed from the mapping fit, reads this input multiplied by a gain field: 1 plus a sustained field at the four ring locations, which differs between the HP location (g_HP) and the three LP locations (g_LP), plus transient fields at the target (g_T) and at the singleton, whose gain can also differ between HP and LP. Shown here is less sustained gain at HP than at LP, relative to LP. HP is at the lower right (red dashed ring). All gains are illustrative and exaggerated for clarity. b, A schematic stretch of a run with illustrative trials: one bar sweep from left to right and four trials, with symbols above their onsets (target: diamond; singleton: dot; HP: red ring; pRF: teal circle). For three pRFs, the rows show the stimulus reaching the pRF (bar and items), the gain it sees, and the predicted BOLD response after the HRF, with and without gain. Rows share their scale across columns.
